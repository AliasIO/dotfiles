# Evidence and emerging formats

Reviewed 2026-09-05. These are research and verification pointers, not performance promises.

## Historical GEO experiment

Aggarwal et al., [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), KDD 2024, evaluated changes to content under the paper's generative-engine benchmarks and visibility measures. The reported improvements depend on the method and evaluation setting. They do not establish a guaranteed traffic, conversion, or citation increase for a present-day engine. Inspect the exact table, metric, baseline, and engine before quoting a result; do not reuse a simplified method-by-method percentage table as universal guidance.

## Current platform guidance

Check the target engine's current official documentation before implementation. Google's [AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-features) is an official starting point. For empirical third-party studies, record publication date, query sample, locale, engine/version, metric, and limitations; distinguish correlation from intervention results.

## Emerging formats

Treat `llms.txt`, Open Knowledge Format, machine-readable page variants, and commerce-agent protocols as optional integration experiments. Verify the format's primary specification, actual consumer support, and the user's use case before recommending implementation. A proposed or parsable file is not evidence that a search engine uses it for ranking. Prefer an inexpensive bounded trial with measurable benefits; keep human-visible content authoritative and avoid duplicate facts drifting between formats.
