# Decision analysis

Frame the alternatives and the outcome that would change the user's choice. Keep exploratory work bounded to hypotheses and comparisons that can affect that decision.

Compare groups at a compatible grain, denominator, window and baseline. High total usage may reflect population size; check normalized intensity, growth, coverage and concentration before calling a segment the best opportunity. Keep missing/unknown groups visible when they can change the result.

Size the actionable opportunity using observed population, rate, value, cost and capacity where relevant. Avoid treating a whole market, correlated behavior or a top-line total as immediately addressable impact. Distinguish confidence in the measured facts from uncertainty in an intervention's effects.

Select useful lenses such as current scale, momentum, breadth, concentration, efficiency, addressability, differentiated needs, substitution, risk or missing coverage. These are options, not a required checklist. Test an alternative explanation or sensitivity when it could reverse the recommendation.

Connect the findings to the actual available lever and constraints. Give a recommendation proportional to the evidence, explain what could change it, and preserve unresolved conflicts. Do not force a decisive conclusion when the evidence supports only a provisional direction.
